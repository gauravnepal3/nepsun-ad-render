Twelve things I learned repotting a fiddle leaf fig

September 2, 2026 · 8 min read · Home

The fig came with the flat. The previous tenant left it by the window with a note that said "she's fine, don't move her," which I ignored within a week, and which I have been apologising for ever since.

Two years on it is six feet tall and has outgrown the pot it arrived in. Here is what happened when I finally did something about that.

1. The root ball was a brick

Not a metaphor. It came out of the pot in one piece, held its shape on the floor, and made a sound when I tapped it. There was almost no soil left — just roots, circling the inside of the pot the way a dog circles before lying down, except the dog eventually stops.

2. You have to cut it

Every guide says to loosen the roots. What they mean is: take a clean knife and slice into the outside of the ball in four places, top to bottom, about an inch deep. It feels like vandalism. It is the single most important step, because a root that is circling will keep circling forever unless something interrupts it.

3. Two inches, not six

The new pot should be about two inches wider than the old one. I bought one that was six inches wider, because the plant is large and the logic seemed sound, and I spent the next month with soil that stayed wet in the middle where no root had reached yet.

4. The soil should look wrong

Good fig soil looks too chunky to be soil. Mine is roughly half bagged potting mix, a quarter orchid bark, and a quarter perlite. When you water it, the water should be out the bottom in about four seconds. If it pools on top, you have made mud, and mud is how the roots rot.

5. It will drop leaves and that is not a verdict

Four leaves, over nine days, all from the bottom. I read every forum thread that has ever been written on the subject, which is a mistake I recommend against, and all of them said the same thing in different fonts: wait.

A fiddle leaf fig responds to change roughly three weeks after the change, which means every conclusion you draw in week one is about something you did last month.

6. Do not fertilise for a month

Fresh mix has enough in it, the cut roots cannot take it up, and the salts build up in soil that is not yet being drained by a full root system. I learned this the second time, not the first.

7. Rotate it a quarter turn every week

Otherwise you end up with a plant that is a flat panel of leaves facing the window and a bare stick facing the room. Mark the pot with a pencil so you know where you were.

8–11. The quick ones

Water when the top two inches are dry, not on a schedule. Dust the leaves — they are enormous and they are the entire point. Do not mist; it does nothing for humidity and does quite a lot for fungus. And keep it away from the radiator, which will cook one side of it slowly enough that you will not notice until it is done.

12. It forgives you

Three weeks after the repot, almost to the day, it pushed out a new leaf at the top that was noticeably bigger than the one below it. Then another. It is now taller than the window frame and I have started thinking about where it goes next, which is how this whole thing began.

The note is still on the windowsill. I have not thrown it out.
